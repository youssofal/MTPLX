# MTPLX 2.13.0

MTPLX 2.13.0 adds Inkling 2 Mini, Thinking Machines Lab's open-weights model for coding, agents and images. On a MacBook Pro M5 Max, Inkling 2 Mini Bare Speed writes 98.2 tokens a second through `mtplx serve`, and every pack reads screenshots with the model's whole vision tower. The app and `mtplx start` offer it on Macs with 24 GB of memory or more (32 GB or more on M1 and M2 Macs), choose the pack for your chip and memory, and open Pi, OpenCode or Hermes on it.

It also brings a new Settings window, your decode speed in the menu bar, tool-call arguments handed to clients exactly as the model wrote them, and prompt cache, memory and reliability fixes for the models 2.12.3 already served.

## What changed

### Inkling 2 Mini

- **A new model.** Inkling 2 Mini (Inkling-2-Mini on Hugging Face) is Thinking Machines Lab's open-weights model for coding, agents and images, released on 20 November 2026 under the Apache License 2.0. Its model card gives 26.4 billion parameters with 3.4 billion active for each token, 8 of 128 experts per token, 12 middle layers that run three times, and a context window of up to 1M tokens. It reads images natively.

- **Three packs, each with an FP16 build for M1 and M2 Macs.** Bare Speed (16.0 GB) keeps the official 4-bit experts and stores the attention, the output head and the embedding in 4 bits. Optimized Speed (17.2 GB) stores the attention and the output head in 8 bits and keeps the original embedding. Optimized Quality (20.2 GB) is the official weights unchanged. The FP16 builds store every 16-bit tensor as float16, since M1 and M2 Macs have no native bfloat16, and keep every 4-bit and 8-bit code as in the parent. Every pack carries the model's whole vision tower and its draft head. They need MTPLX 2.13 or later.

- **Which Mac gets which pack.** The app's model picker, its first-run screen and `mtplx start` read your chip and memory and offer only the packs that fit. On a 24 GB Mac with an M3 or later, Bare Speed comes second, after Bonsai 2 27B. On 32 and 36 GB Macs the three packs come right after Qwen 3.8 27B Optimized Speed, and from 48 GB they sit near the end of the list. M1 and M2 Macs get the FP16 builds in the same places from 32 GB. No pack fits a 16 GB Mac: Bare Speed's weights alone are 16.0 GB.

- **The model's own kernels and draft head.** Inkling 2 Mini runs on Metal kernels written for it. Each round of drafting and checking runs on the GPU as one recorded program, and the model's own multi-token-prediction head drafts several tokens that the full model checks in one pass. Drafts are kept with the probability-ratio rule, so the output follows the model's own distribution at any temperature. MTPLX chooses how many tokens to draft in each round from what this Mac's rounds cost, timed when the model loads.

- **Faster on text the prompt already holds.** When an agent rewrites a file or repeats a patch, MTPLX proposes up to 24 tokens at once from the prompt and checks them in one round. On a rewrite of a 315-line file on Bare Speed it wrote 114.9 tok/s, against 89.8 with this off, with the Mac at heavy thermal pressure; at nominal pressure the same rewrite ran at 180.1 tok/s. On text that matches the prompt only briefly it costs a little: about 0.9% over a 13-minute Pi session.

- **Faster prefill on M5 Macs.** On an M5 Mac with at least 12 GB of GPU memory to spare beyond the model, a prompt is read 2,048 tokens at a time instead of 512, prompts of 4,096 tokens and more use a new attention launch on the GPU's tensor units, and Optimized Quality's last four layers multiply their 8-bit experts there too (8.6 ms instead of 63.5 ms for each of those layers in a 2,048-token step). On a MacBook Pro M5 Max, Bare Speed reads a 4,142-token prompt at 2,466 tok/s (the mean of three runs). Reading a prompt in larger pieces adds the same numbers in a different order, and on this model, as with any engine, two correct ways of reading a prompt can lead to differently worded answers.

- **M3 and M4 Macs.** They take a code path without the M5's tensor units. On an M5 Max forced onto that path, Bare Speed generated 106.1 tok/s on a cached conversation and answered a follow-up's first token in 0.06 s. An M3 or M4 has less memory bandwidth than an M5 Max, so expect lower figures there.

- **Faster reading of long prompts on M1 to M4 Macs.** Macs without the M5's tensor units now read the attention of a prompt of 4,096 tokens or more in one launch on the GPU, and past 18,432 tokens they keep reading 2,048 tokens at a time instead of dropping to 512, once a check at load has passed. On an M5 Max forced onto that code path, in two pairs of runs, Bare Speed read a 4,179-token prompt at 812 and 903 tok/s instead of 745 and 813, and a 16,467-token prompt at 801 and 845 instead of 699 and 695. The first token came 0.46 and 0.51 s sooner at 4K and 3.0 and 4.2 s sooner at 16K. Peak memory was flat. An M1 to M4 has less memory bandwidth than an M5 Max, so expect lower figures there.

- **A prompt is read even where MTPLX's own prefill kernel cannot run.** If a Mac cannot build MTPLX's prefill kernel, MTPLX now falls back to MLX's own kernels for that step instead of failing the request.

- **The model's own sampler.** Temperature 1.0 over the whole distribution, from the model's own generation settings, on the app, `mtplx start` and bare `mtplx serve`. A client that sends its own sampler keeps it. The app's Pi, OpenCode and Hermes buttons now start Inkling 2 Mini with its own tool-call format, chat template and sampler, the same settings `mtplx start` and `mtplx serve` use.

- **Every turn reads only its new tokens.** A follow-up, a tool result, a turn after the server restarts (from the SSD cache; OpenCode is the exception, under Known issues), a turn that goes back to another branch of the conversation, and a conversation a client resends without its id all resume from the saved conversation. In a Pi session, the turns after a 28,000-token and a 35,000-token file write read the whole answer again (65.5 s and 128.9 s to the first token); in a replay of those requests they now read 11 tokens each. Going back to the main thread after a side branch read 9,634 tokens again (11.0 s to the first token) and now starts from that branch's saved state (in tests). Hermes's first turn after a restart read 14 of its 5,229 prompt tokens, 0.44 s to the first token. The app's Pi, Chat and Web UI buttons keep the SSD cache on for Inkling 2 Mini, as `mtplx start` does.

- **Images take the same path as text.** Image turns use the prompt cache, the fast decode path and the memory plan. In Pi through bare `mtplx serve`, the tool round after a screenshot turn read 11 new tokens of 1,872, and the follow-up 24 of 1,935.

- **Memory.** A conversation is kept once, prompt and answer together, instead of beside a second copy (the copy cost 2.28 GB at the start of a 64K answer on Bare Speed). On a 24 GB Mac a conversation keeps one restore point instead of eight (each is 180 MB on Inkling 2 Mini), and the memory plan charges each request the working memory it was measured to need. A prompt longer than the window your Mac's memory allows is now told to shorten or compact the conversation, instead of to raise a setting that would make the Mac swap.

- **A folder named like an Inkling 2 Mini pack is served as that pack only when it is that pack.** Each pack's own `mtplx-pack.json` now has to say it is that build before a folder found by the pack's name is used. Another build kept under the same name (an older build of your own, say) is named in the message, and the published pack is downloaded instead.

- **`mtplx forge` builds the packs.** `mtplx forge inkling` rebuilds each pack on your own Mac from the official weights, and `mtplx forge fp16` makes the M1 and M2 build of a pack. Both check the vision tower and the draft head against the official release and leave nothing behind when a check fails. `mtplx inspect` now reports the Inkling packs as image packs.

### Agent clients and the server

- **The Responses API accepts the Codex desktop app's requests.** Every turn the Codex app (26.1106) sent to `/v1/responses` was refused with a 400. Its reasoning summary setting, its added tools and its namespaced tools are now accepted, and images in Responses input reach the model (#686 by @LivenseAI).

- **Choose where Pi, OpenCode and Hermes open.** Each opens in any installed terminal app or, for OpenCode and Hermes, in its own app. Pick it per client in Settings > Clients, or with `mtplx config set pi_opens_in Ghostty`; `mtplx start --cli` and `--app` override it once.

- **`mtplx start` uses the app's server instead of loading a second copy.** With the app running, `mtplx start opencode`, `hermes` or another client found its own port free and loaded the model a second time (an 87 GB Flash-Next pack cannot fit twice on a 128 GB Mac). It now uses the running server and checks that server's model instead of asking for one or offering a download. `MTPLX_START_ATTACH_PROBE=off` turns this off. The app's Play does the same beside a server started from the terminal.

- **Claude Code's tool search works against the local server.** Claude Code sends every MCP tool definition up front unless its tool search is on (`ENABLE_TOOL_SEARCH=true`): 820 tools made a 254,636-token first prompt. Where the served model's chat template lists tools one definition per line, the server now keeps the deferred tools out of the prompt and writes each one into the conversation where Claude Code loads it, so the cached prompt stays the same.

- **Claude Code's system context reaches the model as system context.** Claude Code sends its session context as a system message after the first user message, and MTPLX folded it into your message as if you had written it: in one session, a question asked "without searching" made the model search the Mac's project files anyway. It now reaches the model marked as system context.

- **Claude Code requests start sooner.** A check for a repeated message compared every split of every user message, which took 75 to 105 ms before each Claude Code request reached the GPU. On Claude Code's captured requests it now takes 2.3 to 3.2 ms instead of 40 to 41.

- **A client's limit on its own thinking is honoured.** `thinking_budget`, `thinking_token_budget`, `reasoning.max_tokens` and Anthropic's `thinking.budget_tokens` were accepted and ignored. The model now closes its reasoning at that budget, on both endpoints. Claude Code sends one; Pi, OpenCode and Hermes do not. `MTPLX_THINKING_BUDGET=off` turns every budget off.

- **Cancel works on `/v1/completions`** (#687 by @SuperMarioYL). A cancel answered `cancelled: false`, and a client that gave up kept the model busy until `max_tokens`. Completions requests now stop on a cancel or a disconnect, as chat requests do.

- **The dashboard follows requests that do not stream** (#689 by @jvmenen). A non-streaming request showed no live progress until it finished: a 25-second answer sent 0 progress events. Chat, messages, responses and completions requests now update the live gauge either way.

- **Short conversations keep their session.** A client that sends no session id and re-encodes its tool calls, such as Hermes, re-read its last answer on every turn until the conversation passed 4,096 tokens. It now stays in its session.

- **Qwen 3.5 and 3.6 keep the round's reasoning through Claude Code's tool steps** (#570 by @jvmenen). Claude Code's bookkeeping block after a tool result was read as a new question, so every tool step dropped the round's reasoning, and the end-of-turn save was refused.

- **Thinking answers reach the client sooner.** The stream held the last 32 characters of reasoning, so every thinking answer's first words waited seven to nine tokens (0.128 s of the 0.15 s to the first visible text on an Inkling 2 Mini warm turn). Reasoning is now sent as it is written, with the same bytes as before. Measured at the client through `mtplx serve`, at Nominal: on Qwen 3.8 Flash-Next Optimized Speed a repeated prompt's first token came in 24 ms instead of 117 ms, and an agent's next two turns in 199 and 221 ms instead of 281 and 443 ms (two runs of each version); on Qwen 3.8 27B Optimized Speed, 15 ms instead of 142 ms (three runs).

- **`mtplx start webui`** works, as the app's Web UI row does.

- **Sampling controls a client sends now apply.** Requests that send `logit_bias`, `min_p` or `repetition_penalty` now get them on Inkling 2 Mini and on packs served without drafting; 2.12.3 accepted them and dropped them on every model. The Qwen and Gemma packs' drafting lanes still ignore all three. A malformed value is refused with a 400 naming the field: `logit_bias` maps token ids to numbers from -100 to 100, and `min_p` is between 0 and 1.

### Tool calls

- **Tool-call arguments arrive exactly as the model wrote them** (#691, reported with a reproduction script by @robin1333k). Every file an agent wrote through MTPLX lost its last newline, on every client and every Qwen-format pack: Pi, OpenCode and Hermes on `/v1/chat/completions`, Claude Code on `/v1/messages` and Codex on `/v1/responses`. Leading indentation and trailing blank lines were changed the same way, a JSON file or a file holding only a number was refused, and the call came back to the next turn in a different form, so that turn read it again. Each argument now arrives as the model generated it, and arguments keep the model's order. `MTPLX_TOOL_ARGUMENTS_EXACT=0` restores the old handling.

### Prompt cache and memory, every model

- **A follow-up after a long answer resumes from that answer.** At the end of a turn the server compared what the model wrote with its guess of how the client would send it back, and when only the guess disagreed it threw the state away: the next Pi turn on 2.12.3 read 31,920 tokens again and took 32.9 s to its first text. The state that wrote the answer is now kept. On Flash-Next an answer from a client without a session id is kept without a second copy too (that copy was 4.24 GB at 131,000 tokens). `MTPLX_POSTCOMMIT_KEEP_LIVE_STATE=0` restores the old rule. Keeping that state uses more memory, inside the same cache budget: in a test of four conversations through `mtplx serve` (a 4K and a 16K chat, three tool turns and an image), Qwen 3.8 27B Optimized Speed peaked at 29.55 GB of model memory instead of 27.75 GB (27.62 GB with the old rule), and Qwen 3.8 Flash-Next at 90.5 GB in both versions. Before it would refuse a request, the cache frees that memory, idle conversations first. The Flash-Next process as a whole peaked at 97.56 to 97.99 GB instead of 96.23 to 96.35 GB.

- **Less work at the end of each answer.** On Flash-Next, saving a turn no longer re-renders and re-encodes the whole conversation (0.12 to 0.29 s at the end of every answer at 101,000 to 157,000 tokens). On every model the token hashes are computed in C: saving after a 42,626-token answer went from 178 to 223 ms to 3.1 to 4.3 ms (a Pi tool round that arrived 0.19 s after such an answer had waited 0.69 s for the save). Finding a conversation in the SSD cache took 343 ms for a 56,000-token prompt in a cache of 5.7 million tokens, and takes 8.6 ms (#629 by @jvmenen). Comparing an 8,000-token prompt with the saved conversations takes 1.2 ms instead of 4.0 (#628 by @jvmenen), and a turn at 156,000 tokens with 18 screenshots spends 20 ms instead of 34 locating its images in the prompt.

- **A new session resumes at the end of the system prompt it shares.** Every new agent session in a project, every subagent and the first turn after a compaction send the same system prompt, then differ. On models with recurrent layers, such as Flash-Next, such a turn resumed at 4,096 tokens and read the rest of an 8,000-token system prompt again. It now resumes at the end of any system prompt of 2,048 tokens or more. `MTPLX_SYSTEM_PROMPT_CHECKPOINT=0` turns this off.

- **After a cancelled answer, the next message resumes from memory.** In a Pi session at 118,096 tokens, the message after a cancelled answer was read back from the SSD cache (first text after 1.83 s instead of about 0.5 s) and held the conversation twice. It now resumes in memory where the cancelled turn began.

- **A short prompt sent again is served from the cache.** On Flash-Next a 76-token prompt sent three times was read again each time. A prompt sent again is now served from the first answer's saved state, on models that save a restore point at the end of each prompt.

- **Qwen 3.5 and 3.6 follow-ups read less with thinking on.** These templates drop the previous turn's reasoning from the next prompt. A follow-up that arrived while MTPLX was already reading that history in the background cancelled the work and read 12,245 and 19,832 tokens again in a Pi session on Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B (first text after 4.85 and 8.08 s); it now waits for that work. The restore point at the start of the current turn is also kept through its tool loop, where tool results used to push it out (one turn resumed 4,051 tokens earlier, about 1.5 s). `MTPLX_POSTCOMMIT_ADOPTION=0` turns the wait off.

- **Follow-ups continue in the conversation's own cache on every client.** Every client's follow-up now continues in the conversation's own cache instead of a copy of the whole conversation beside it; before, only the app's agent buttons did.

- **The first long prompt after a start is priced right.** On Flash-Next, the memory guard billed the first long prompt of each server process as a second copy of its state: 6.28 GB too much at 193,533 tokens, and a cold 247,048-token prompt that fits was refused. It is now billed as every later prompt is.

- **A conversation restored from the SSD cache is held once.** After a 247,034-token Flash-Next answer, a follow-up restored from the SSD cache grew 16.1 GB for an 8.66 GB conversation and ended in a 507. Each step of the restore that held the conversation twice now holds it once: in tests, rebuilding the conversation peaked at 1.03 times its size instead of 1.79, and the first answer step after it at 1.21 times instead of 2.20.

- **Memory held after a long read is given back sooner.** After a long prefill or a long restore from the SSD cache, MLX kept the buffers it no longer needed until 1,024 tokens into the answer (6.25 to 6.73 GB for about 18 s after a 118,096-token restore). They are now released at the answer's first round.

- **The model stays in GPU memory while the SSD cache writes.** macOS drops a process's GPU working set about 2.5 s after its last GPU work, and saving a long answer to the SSD cache takes seconds of CPU work, so a request that arrived during the write paid about 0.75 to 1.0 s to bring Flash-Next's weights back. MTPLX now keeps them resident during that work.

- **The SSD cache writer lets go of what it wrote.** After each write it kept a whole conversation's encoded bytes in memory while it waited for the next: the server's own memory went from 0.12 GB to 2.2 GB after a 16,000-token turn on Inkling 2 Mini and stayed there. Every model that writes to the SSD cache was affected.

- **A failed save no longer fails a finished answer** (#696, reported by @menega). With a small session budget, a save that dropped all of its restore points raised an error, which reached the client as a stream error after the answer had arrived in full, and the client asked for the whole answer again. The save now stores the conversation without them, and any error in a save is recorded in the request log while the answer ends normally.

- **An explicit RAM session cache size no longer throws the conversation away** (#690, reported with an analysis and a patch by @robin1333k). With `MTPLX_SESSION_BANK_MAX_BYTES`, or the app's RAM session cache field, set below the per-conversation limit, each new save was larger than the whole cache, so it was dropped from memory as soon as it was stored, and the next turn read everything again. Such a save now drops its oldest restore points until it fits.

- **Hermes and other clients that send no session id no longer read the model's last answer again on every turn of a short conversation.** A conversation under 4,096 tokens that the client resent with part of the model's own answer lost its session, so each turn read that answer again until the conversation passed 4,096 tokens; it now keeps its session.

### Other models

- **Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B reads images at their positions.** Its image tokens were placed by their order in the prompt instead of their place in the picture, which the dense Qwen packs have done right since September. Conversations with images saved before the update read their prompt once.

- **The Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B Optimized Speed and Optimized Balance packs draft better.** One norm in their draft head was stored without its +1, so every draft step read its input at about half scale. MTPLX now repairs it when the pack loads and says so in the startup log, `/health` and the request log. The output's distribution is the model's own either way; the draft head keeps more of its tokens (tokens written per round went from 2.63 to 3.54 in a 2,048-token test answer, with the draft depth fixed at three). `MTPLX_DRAFT_HEAD_NORM_REPAIR=0` loads the pack as stored.

- **A seed gives the same answer again on Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B.** Its default speculative lane drew tokens from a generator seeded by the clock, so one seed gave a different answer on every request. It now draws from the request's seed on the Optimized Speed pack and its FP16 build; the answers follow the same distribution as before, but which answer a given seed gives differs from 2.12.3.

- **A Mac newer than the M5 takes the M5's paths** (#695, reported by @audenvenn). An M6 was classified as "unknown" and got the oldest paths.

- **The kernel self-check names an unsupported weight layout** (#677, reported by @energypantry). On an MXFP8 pack the startup log said "qmm_m4 raised" four times; it now says in one line that the fast matrix kernels do not support that layout.

### The app

- **The reasoning effort control now reads from low to high on every model**, as Inkling 2 Mini's does; the Qwen models used to list theirs from extra high down.

- **After your third model load, the app asks once whether everything is working well**, with Star on GitHub and File an issue as the answers; whichever you pick, or if you close it, it never asks again, and the app itself sends nothing.

- **A new Settings window.** Settings has a sidebar with eight sections, search over every setting, and one save bar. It opens from a gear in the top bar, the app menu or Command-comma. The popover beside it is now "Model controls", and the performance rows say in plain words what they do.

- **Your decode speed in the menu bar** (#692, requested by @ryanwwest). The menu bar shows MTPLX's state and, while a request decodes, its speed. A click opens the quick controls. It is on by default: in five pairs of runs with it on and off, it cost nothing measurable during a decode. Settings > General turns it off.

- **The app uses less while it waits.** Its window no longer redraws at the screen's refresh rate when nothing changes: 253 to 7 wakeups a second with the Live tab showing and nothing running, 273 to 4 minimized. The speedometer stops drawing when it comes to rest and draws nothing while the window is hidden, and the dashboard's request rows are sent only when they change.

- **Readouts that say what happened.** Activity shows how long a turn thought before it answered ("thought for 34 s, 1,850 tokens"). A prompt read while macOS slowed the chip for heat is marked, and the average prefill is weighted by tokens with how many reads were slowed: in one 2.12.3 Pi session, 50 of 57 requests were read under thermal pressure. The memory card shows the memory guard's own limit and the room left. The request log records each turn's thinking time, what its images cost, and the worst thermal state during its prefill.

- **Model folders are shared with the command line** (#388, #466). The app and `mtplx` read and write the same model folders in `~/.mtplx/config.toml`. `mtplx folders` lists them with their packs, and a folder on a drive that is not plugged in stays listed as not connected, in the app's model picker too. A model whose drive is unplugged is never downloaded again: the app and `mtplx start` ask you to plug the drive in.

- **The fans are left alone when they are not the app's.** Stop and Quit reset the fans to automatic even in a copy of the app that never ran a server, or after a server that ran with Default fans, which undid another tool's or another MTPLX copy's setting.

- **The dashboard's cards share one style**, with the Live tab's headers on Activity and System.

### Pack tools

- **`mtplx forge census`** lists every reason a new checkpoint cannot be built yet, and **`mtplx forge day-zero`** runs a release day from a repository to packs, one resumable step at a time. Every draft-head norm is checked against the published value.

- **A quality tool for packs.** `mtplx.benchmarks.quality` measures what a pack costs against the full-precision model: 20 checkable tasks, the divergence from the reference model, and draft acceptance. Forge recipes can choose each group's rounding range for the least error.

- **Publishing a pack keeps its tokenizer intact.** `mtplx forge publish` (the app's Forge tab runs it at its Publish step) and `mtplx model publish-check --scrub` treated any text starting with `~` as a home folder and rewrote the tokenizer entries that begin with it (seven in Inkling 2 Mini's `tokenizer.json`), so a published pack's tokenizer was broken. Only `~/...` and `~user/...` count as home paths now.

- **`mtplx forge verify` stamps the fastest verified draft depth and a served id** on packs built on your Mac (#575 by @jvmenen).

### Dependencies

- **Pillow 13 on every install channel.** pip, Homebrew and a new app runtime already installed Pillow 13 while the lockfile kept 12.3. Pillow 13 changes the pixels of an image that the vision tower's grid enlarges (Pi's 1500x1420 screenshots), and every Inkling 2 Mini check ran on it, so every channel now installs Pillow 13 (`pillow>=13,<14`). An app runtime moves to it at its next update.

- transformers is allowed up to 5.22 (#684): a 40-message agent transcript at every reasoning level gave the same token ids as under 5.16 on 19 pack tokenizers, 380 of 380 cases. filelock 4.2.1.

- The wheel carries MTPLX's native engine, built against MLX 0.32.2. An install without the build tools still works and serves from MLX.

## Experimental, off by default

These are built and tested, and stay off until what each one changes is accepted.

- **Drafts from a smaller vocabulary** (`MTPLX_INKLING_DRAFT_TABLE=<ranking.json>`): Inkling 2 Mini's draft head scores only the most frequent tokens. The output still follows the model's distribution, but which tokens a given seed produces changes, and that is not accepted for this release.
- **Copy rounds of 9 to 32 tokens decided on the GPU** (`MTPLX_INKLING_WIDE_DECISION=1`): the same kind of change as above.
- **A cost gate for copy rounds** (`MTPLX_CONTEXT_COPY_GATE=1`): it skips copy rounds that are too short to pay, but it cost 3.4% in a replay of a long Pi session.
- **A reordered long-history attention for decode** (`MTPLX_INKLING_ATTEND_C=1`): it changes the rounding slightly and costs 2.2 ms on each one-token step at 64,000 tokens.
- **The round that writes its own kept tokens** (`MTPLX_INKLING_ROUND_COMMIT=1`): it was not faster on warm turns and was slower on cold ones in a served A/B, because of about 430 small cache writes.
- **Width-independent prefill** (`MTPLX_INKLING_PROMPT_FAST=invariant`, or `=experts` for its expert part alone): no comparison with an exact reference exists yet.

The fast prefill above is on by default. `MTPLX_INKLING_PROMPT_FAST=off` returns to 512-token reads, and `MTPLX_INKLING_PROMPT_FLASH=off` to the standard attention.

## Known issues

- **No Inkling 2 Mini pack fits a 16 GB Mac on any chip** (Bare Speed's weights alone are 16.0 GB), and M1 and M2 Macs get one from 32 GB.
- **On a 16 GB Mac with other apps open, the recommended pack can refuse turns with "insufficient memory" until you close some apps**; this is as in 2.12.3, and the fix is planned for 2.13.1.
- **On a 32 GB Mac, Qwen 3.8 27B Optimized Speed gets an 8,192-token window while Bonsai 2 27B gets far more**; this is as in 2.12.3, and the fix is planned for 2.13.1.
- **The FP16 builds have not run on an M1 or M2 Mac yet:** all three passed their checks on the code path an M1 or M2 takes, on an M5 Max, and no speed is listed for them.
- **On a 24 GB Mac the window is 24,576 tokens and each conversation keeps one restore point**, so a prompt that changes before its last 64 tokens (an edited earlier message, a regenerated reply) is restored from the SSD cache or read again.
- **In Pi, the first turn after `/new` on Inkling 2 Mini reads much of Pi's system prompt again**: 2,334 and 8,422 of about 10,500 tokens in two tries, 2.8 s and 7.7 s before the first token.
- **A tool call reaches the client only once the model has finished writing it**, so an agent writing a large file shows nothing until the file is complete, as in earlier releases and on the Qwen packs.
- **After the server restarts, OpenCode's next turn reads its whole conversation again** (12.8 s to the first token for 22,070 tokens on Bare Speed, 37.7 s on the code path M3 and M4 Macs take), while Pi, Hermes and the app's Chat pick theirs up from the SSD cache in about a second.
- **If the server restarts while an agent is in a tool loop, the next turn can read the whole conversation again**, because the conversation reaches the SSD cache only when the session pauses (78,836 tokens took 40.4 s in a Pi session on Inkling 2 Mini), as in earlier releases and on the Qwen packs.
- **A conversation the server switched away from with no five-second pause may be missing from the SSD cache after a restart**, so its next turn reads the whole conversation again (about 8 s for 10,642 tokens on Inkling 2 Mini).
- **The visible answer after a model's reasoning starts two to three rounds after its first token**, because the server holds the answer's last 32 characters until it knows they are not the start of a tool call.
- **A short follow-up on Inkling 2 Mini spends about 0.1 s before its first token whatever its length** (11 new tokens took 117 ms in a Pi session).
- **With an explicit RAM session cache size, a conversation whose saved state alone is larger than the whole cache is still dropped from memory until its SSD copy is written** (past about 28,600 tokens at 2 GB on Qwen 3.6 27B).
- **When MTPLX refuses a streamed request for memory, the client shows a server error instead of the refusal**, because the stream has already started; this is not new in 2.13, and the fix is planned for 2.13.1.
- **The app's reasoning effort control offers low, medium, high and extra high for Inkling 2 Mini**; the model's other levels (none, minimal and max) work when a client sends them, as Pi and OpenCode do, but cannot yet be the default in the app or with `mtplx serve --reasoning-effort`.
- **The known issues of 2.12.3 still apply**: see the [2.12.3 release notes](https://github.com/youssofal/MTPLX/releases/tag/v2.12.3).

## Upgrading

Run `brew upgrade mtplx` or `pip install -U mtplx`, or use the app's update check. You don't need to change any settings. To try Inkling 2 Mini, pick it in the app, or run `mtplx pull Youssofal/Inkling-2-Mini-MTPLX-Bare-Speed` and `mtplx start pi --model Youssofal/Inkling-2-Mini-MTPLX-Bare-Speed` (the FP16 build on an M1 or M2: `Youssofal/Inkling-2-Mini-MTPLX-Bare-Speed-FP16`). Inkling 2 Mini packs do not load in MTPLX 2.12 or earlier. Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B conversations with images that were saved before the update read their prompt once.
